RAD 312

⚡ Mains Voltage Compensation

Stable Voltage • Manual & Automatic Compensators • Line-Drop Compensation

📋 One-Sentence Summary

Mains voltage compensation keeps the voltage supplied to the X-ray machine stable despite fluctuations in the incoming supply (outside exposure) and voltage drops caused by high tube current (during exposure).

🧠 Key Memory Trick

OUTSIDE exposure → Mains voltage compensator
DURING exposure → Line-drop compensation transformer
More mA → More current → More voltage drop → More compensation

1️⃣ What is Mains Voltage Compensation?

It is the process of keeping the voltage supplied to an X-ray machine sufficiently stable despite changes in the incoming electrical supply.

🧠 Analogy

Think of water pressure in a pipe.
If pressure becomes too low or too high, the equipment may not work properly.
A pressure-regulating device keeps the pressure near the required level.
A voltage compensator does the same job for electrical voltage.

2️⃣ Why Does Mains Voltage Fluctuate?

A. Fluctuations Outside Exposure

  • Other electrical equipment connected to the same mains supply
  • Inconsistencies in the electricity supplied by the generating station

B. Fluctuations During Exposure

During exposure the X-ray tube demands a large amount of current. This increased demand can cause the supply voltage to drop.

More tube current → greater electrical demand → possible voltage drop

3️⃣ Where is the Compensator Located?

Positioned between the mains supply and the autotransformer:

Mains supply → Mains voltage compensator → Autotransformer → X-ray circuit

It adjusts fluctuations before the voltage reaches the autotransformer.

🧠 Important Point

The compensator does not prevent the mains voltage from changing.
It corrects the changes so that the voltage supplied to the X-ray circuit remains more stable.

4️⃣ Why is Voltage Stability Important?

Voltage fluctuations can cause:

  • Unstable tube current
  • Fluctuating X-ray intensity
  • Underexposed or overexposed images
  • Reduced image consistency
  • Increased wear on components
  • Reduced equipment lifespan
  • Increased maintenance requirements
🧠 Analogy

Imagine taking photographs while the brightness of the flash keeps changing.
One photo may be too dark, the next correct, the next too bright.
The same problem occurs with X-ray production when the electrical supply is unstable.

5️⃣ Compensation Outside Exposure

Manual Voltage Compensator

Common in older X-ray equipment. Consists of:

  • A control knob
  • A voltage meter (moving-iron voltmeter) with a red mark showing the ideal voltage

The control knob selects different tappings on the primary winding of the autotransformer.

Key relationship:
Voltage decreases → reduce selected turns
Voltage increases → increase selected turns

Example 1 – Voltage falls

Normal supply = 240 V → falls to 220 V
Meter needle moves left of the red mark.
Operator selects 220 turns instead of 240 turns.

Example 2 – Voltage rises

Supply increases above normal.
Meter needle moves right of the red mark.
Operator increases the number of selected turns.

6️⃣ Automatic Voltage Compensators

Unlike the manual type, an automatic compensator does not require the radiographer to make continuous adjustments.

It:

  • Detects a change in mains voltage
  • Determines whether voltage has fallen or increased
  • Makes the correction automatically
  • Maintains a more stable voltage to the autotransformer

Uses sensors and control circuits.

Feature Manual Automatic
Adjustment Human operator Automatic system
Detection Operator watches meter Sensors detect change
Common in Older equipment Modern equipment
🧠 Analogy

An automatic water-pressure regulator. You don’t stand beside it turning a knob every time pressure changes — the system detects the change and adjusts itself.

7️⃣ Compensation During Exposure

During exposure the X-ray tube draws a large current (especially at high mA). This produces a voltage drop in the circuit.

Line Drop Compensation Transformer
Purpose: helps maintain stable voltage during exposure.

Why does voltage drop?

Higher tube current → higher electrical load → greater voltage drop

  • Low mA → smaller current demand → smaller voltage drop → less compensation needed
  • High mA → large current demand → larger voltage drop → more compensation needed

Position of the transformer:

Mains supply → Line-drop compensation transformer → Autotransformer → X-ray circuit

It is a small current-type transformer placed in series with the load current.

8️⃣ How the Line-Drop Transformer Works

It produces a compensating voltage related to the current flowing through its primary winding. This voltage offsets the voltage lost because of the load.

Greater current → greater voltage drop → greater compensating voltage
Lower current → smaller voltage drop → smaller compensating voltage

Worked Example 1

Turns ratio = 20 : 1
Primary current = 50 A
Resistor = 2 Ω

Secondary current = 50 A ÷ 20 = 2.5 A
Compensating voltage = 2.5 A × 2 Ω = 5 V

Worked Example 2

Current falls to 30 A

Secondary current = 30 A ÷ 20 = 1.5 A
Compensating voltage = 1.5 A × 2 Ω = 3 V

Load Current Compensating Voltage
50 A 5 V
30 A 3 V
🧠 Analogy

Pushing a shopping trolley.
On a smooth floor → small force needed.
On a rough floor → more force needed.
The line-drop transformer detects increased “resistance” (higher current) and supplies extra assistance (more compensating voltage).

9️⃣ Why Line-Drop Compensation Matters Clinically

Without adequate compensation

Voltage fluctuation → change in tube operation → variation in X-ray beam → inconsistent image → possible repeat exposure → increased patient dose

With compensation

Voltage fluctuation → compensator responds → voltage kept more consistent → more consistent X-ray production → more consistent image quality

⭐ High-Yield Points

  • Mains voltage compensation keeps voltage stable despite supply changes
  • Two situations: Outside exposure & During exposure
  • Outside exposure → Manual or Automatic mains compensator
  • During exposure → Line-drop compensation transformer
  • Manual: operator adjusts turns using meter and knob
  • Automatic: sensors + control circuits adjust automatically
  • Higher mA → greater current → greater voltage drop → greater compensation
  • Example: 50 A → 5 V compensation; 30 A → 3 V compensation (20:1 ratio, 2 Ω)
  • Unstable voltage can cause underexposed/overexposed images and higher patient dose
