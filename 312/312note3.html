RAD 312

⚡ Mains Voltage Compensation

Stable Voltage • Manual & Automatic Compensators • Line-Drop Compensation

📋 One-Sentence Summary

Mains voltage compensation keeps the voltage supplied to the X-ray machine stable despite fluctuations in the incoming supply (outside exposure) and voltage drops caused by high tube current (during exposure).

🧠 Key Memory Trick

OUTSIDE exposure → Mains voltage compensator
DURING exposure → Line-drop compensation transformer
More mA → More current → More voltage drop → More compensation

1️⃣ Introduction to Mains Voltage Compensation

What is mains voltage compensation?

Mains voltage compensation is the process of keeping the voltage supplied to an X-ray machine sufficiently stable despite changes in the incoming electrical supply.

🧠 Analogy

Think of it like water pressure in a pipe. If the water pressure suddenly becomes too low, the equipment may not work properly. If the pressure becomes too high, the equipment can also be affected. A pressure-regulating device helps keep the pressure near the required level. Similarly, a voltage compensator helps the X-ray machine maintain a suitable and stable voltage.

Why does mains voltage fluctuate?

The material identifies two major situations:

A. Fluctuations outside exposure

  • Other electrical equipment connected to the same mains supply
  • Inconsistencies in the electricity supplied by the generating station

B. Fluctuations during exposure

During X-ray exposure, the X-ray tube demands a large amount of current. This increased demand can cause the supply voltage to drop.

More tube current → greater electrical demand → possible voltage drop

These voltage changes can affect X-ray production and image quality.

Where is the mains voltage compensator located?

Mains supply → Mains voltage compensator → Autotransformer → X-ray circuit

Its purpose is to adjust fluctuations before the voltage reaches the autotransformer. The autotransformer can then provide the appropriate voltage to the rest of the X-ray circuit.

🧠 Important Point

The compensator does not prevent the mains voltage from changing. Instead, it corrects the changes so that the voltage supplied to the X-ray circuit remains more stable.

Why is voltage stability important?

The voltage supplied to the X-ray equipment affects the operation of the X-ray tube. Voltage fluctuations can cause:

  • Unstable tube current
  • Fluctuating X-ray intensity
  • Underexposed images
  • Overexposed images
  • Reduced image consistency
  • Increased wear on components
  • Reduced equipment lifespan
  • Increased maintenance requirements
🧠 Simple Analogy

Imagine taking photographs with a camera while the brightness of the flash keeps changing. One photograph might be too dark → correct → too bright → too dark. The same general problem can occur with X-ray production when the electrical supply is unstable.

2️⃣ Compensation Outside Exposure

The material divides voltage compensation into two major situations: Compensation outside exposure and Compensation during exposure. On this page the focus is on manual voltage compensation.

Manual Voltage Compensator

Older X-ray equipment commonly uses a manual mains voltage compensator. It generally consists of:

  • A control knob
  • A voltage readout/meter (moving-iron voltmeter)

There is a red mark on the meter indicating the ideal voltage level at which compensation is not required.

How does the operator adjust it?

The control knob selects different tappings on the primary winding of the autotransformer. In simple terms, the operator changes the number of turns being used to compensate for a change in incoming voltage.

The material gives a useful example of a 1:1 volts-to-turns ratio. Therefore 240 V → 240 turns.

Example 1: Mains voltage falls

Suppose the normal supply is 240 V but it falls to 220 V. The meter needle moves to the left of the red mark. The radiographer then adjusts the compensator so that the autotransformer uses the appropriate number of turns corresponding to the lower voltage: 220 V → 220 turns. This helps maintain the required voltage relationship.

Example 2: Mains voltage increases

Suppose the supply increases above its normal value. The meter needle moves to the right of the red mark. The radiographer increases the selected number of turns to correspond with the increased voltage.

Key relationship:
Voltage decreases → reduce selected turns
Voltage increases → increase selected turns

🧠 Easy way to remember

Think of the compensator as a manual balancing system:
Low voltage → adjust downward
High voltage → adjust upward

3️⃣ Automatic Voltage Compensators

The diagram on this page shows the relationship between major components of the X-ray circuit, including AC minor kV selector, major kV selector, autotransformer, step-up transformer, exposure switch and timing system. The diagram illustrates how the voltage-control components are incorporated into the X-ray circuit.

Simplified circuit concept:
MAINS → Voltage Compensation → Autotransformer → kV Selection → Step-up Transformer → X-ray Tube

Automatic Voltage Compensators

Unlike the manual compensator, an automatic voltage compensator does not require the radiographer to continually make adjustments. It:

  • Detects a change in mains voltage
  • Determines whether the voltage has fallen or increased
  • Makes an adjustment automatically
  • Maintains a more stable voltage supplied to the autotransformer

Main components involved: Sensors and control circuits. These detect voltage changes and make the necessary correction.

🧠 Analogy

Imagine an automatic water-pressure regulator. You don’t have to stand beside it and turn a knob whenever water pressure changes. The system detects the change and adjusts itself. An automatic voltage compensator works on a similar principle.

Feature Manual compensator Automatic compensator
Adjustment Human operator Automatic system
Detection Operator observes meter Sensors detect change
Response Manual Automatic
Common association Older equipment Modern equipment
Useful when Operator can monitor supply Frequent/unpredictable fluctuations

The material particularly associates automatic compensators with advanced X-ray systems and environments where power fluctuations are frequent or unpredictable.

4️⃣ Compensation During Exposure

This section introduces a different problem. The problem is no longer simply fluctuation in the incoming mains supply. During an exposure, the X-ray tube itself draws a substantial current, especially at high mA settings. This can produce a voltage drop in the circuit.

The material therefore introduces the Line Drop Compensation Transformer. Its purpose is to help maintain stable voltage during exposure.

Why does voltage drop during exposure?

Higher tube current → higher electrical load → greater voltage drop

  • Low mA → Smaller current demand → Smaller voltage drop → Less compensation needed
  • High mA → Large current demand → Larger voltage drop → More compensation needed

The voltage drop can affect important X-ray components such as the filament transformer, rectifiers and high-voltage transformer. Consequently, unstable voltage can affect the consistency of X-ray production.

Position of the Line Drop Compensation Transformer

The material describes it as a small current-type transformer placed in series with the load current. Its position is between:

Mains supply → Line drop compensation transformer → Autotransformer

Because it is in the primary circuit, it can respond to changes in current demand.

Simplified idea:
Mains → Line-drop compensating transformer → Autotransformer → X-ray circuit → X-ray tube

5️⃣ How the Line Drop Compensation Transformer Works

This is one of the most important pages because it provides a numerical example.

Basic principle

The compensating transformer produces a compensating voltage related to the current flowing through its primary winding. The compensating voltage helps offset the voltage lost because of the load.

Greater current → greater voltage drop → greater compensating voltage
Lower current → smaller voltage drop → smaller compensating voltage

Worked Example

The material gives:

  • Turns ratio = 20 : 1
  • Primary current = 50 A
  • Resistor = 2 Ω

Step 1 — Determine the secondary current
Using the turns ratio 20 : 1 → Secondary current = 50 A ÷ 20 = 2.5 A

Step 2 — Calculate the voltage
Using Ohm’s law: Voltage = Current × Resistance = 2.5 A × 2 Ω = 5 V

This 5 V acts to compensate for the voltage drop associated with the 50 A load.

Second Example — Current falls to 30 A

Now suppose the load current falls from 50 A → 30 A. Using the same 20 : 1 ratio:

Secondary current = 30 A ÷ 20 = 1.5 A
Compensating voltage = 1.5 A × 2 Ω = 3 V

Load current Compensating voltage
50 A 5 V
30 A 3 V

The key relationship:
Higher load current → higher booster voltage
Lower load current → lower booster voltage
This allows the system to compensate according to the size of the voltage drop.

🧠 A Simple Analogy

Imagine you are pushing a shopping trolley. On a smooth floor: Small force → trolley moves easily. On a rough floor: More resistance → more force needed. The line-drop compensation transformer is somewhat like a system that detects the increased resistance and supplies additional assistance.
In the X-ray circuit: More current → greater voltage drop. The compensating transformer responds by providing more compensating voltage.

6️⃣ Importance of Line Drop Compensation

The final page emphasizes why all of this matters clinically. Voltage stability during an X-ray exposure is important because changes in tube voltage can affect tube current (mA) and consequently the intensity and quality of the X-ray beam.

Without adequate compensation

Voltage fluctuation → Change in tube operation → Variation in X-ray beam → Inconsistent image → Possible repeat exposure → Increased patient radiation dose

With compensation

Voltage fluctuation → Compensator responds → Voltage maintained more consistently → More consistent X-ray production → More consistent image quality

The material specifically notes that unstable X-ray production can result in underexposed or overexposed images, potentially requiring repeat exposures and increasing patient radiation dose.

🧠 Exam-Focused Summary

  • What is mains voltage compensation? It is the process of correcting variations in the mains voltage supplied to an X-ray machine so that the equipment receives a more stable voltage.
  • Why is it necessary? Voltage fluctuations can affect tube current, X-ray intensity, image quality, equipment operation and component lifespan.
  • When is compensation required? Outside exposure (handled by a mains voltage compensator) and During exposure (handled by a line drop compensation transformer).
  • What is a manual voltage compensator? A manually operated system in which the radiographer observes the voltage meter and adjusts a control knob to select appropriate autotransformer tappings.
  • What is an automatic voltage compensator? A system that uses sensors and control circuits to detect mains voltage variations and automatically make corrections.
  • What causes voltage drop during exposure? The high current demand of the X-ray tube, particularly at high mA settings.
  • What does the line drop compensation transformer do? It generates a compensating/boosting voltage related to the load current to offset voltage drops during exposure.
  • What is the relationship between current and compensation? Higher current → greater voltage drop → greater compensation. Lower current → smaller voltage drop → smaller compensation.
  • Numerical example to remember: Turns ratio = 20:1, Primary current = 50 A, Resistance = 2 Ω → Compensating voltage = 5 V. If current falls to 30 A → Compensating voltage = 3 V.
