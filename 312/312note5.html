RAD 312

⚡ Fuses, Switches & Circuit Breakers

Electrical Protection • Slow/Fast Fuses • Thermal & Electromagnetic Breakers

📋 One-Sentence Summary

Fuses, circuit breakers and switches protect X-ray equipment from excessive current by interrupting the circuit when necessary, while switches also control the making and breaking of electrical connections.

🧠 Ultimate Memory Line

Fuse melts • Breaker trips • Switch controls • Relay uses magnetism
Thermal = Heat → Bend → Trip
Electromagnetic = Magnet → Pull → Trip

1️⃣ Why Protection is Needed

X-ray equipment is supplied from the electrical mains. Voltage spikes and fluctuations can cause current to rise higher than components can safely handle.

Excessive current can:

  • Overheat components
  • Damage equipment
  • Cause failure
  • Create electrical safety hazards

The two major protective devices are fuses and circuit breakers. Their main purpose is to disconnect the equipment when excessive current occurs.

🧠 Analogy

Electrical current is like water flowing through a pipe. If too much water suddenly enters, the pipe could burst. A protective device acts like an automatic valve that stops the flow before serious damage occurs.

2️⃣ Fuses

A fuse is a protective device that protects a circuit from overcurrent by melting and breaking the circuit when excessive current flows.

How a fuse works

Current through the fuse element generates heat. If the current becomes excessively high, the heat melts the element (“the fuse blows”).

Excessive current → Fuse heats → Fuse melts → Circuit opens → Equipment is protected

Correct connection

A fuse must always be connected in series with the circuit it protects.

Power supply → Fuse → Equipment

If connected in parallel, current could still flow through another path and protection would fail.

Types of fuses

  • Resettable (PTC) fuses – can return to operation after cooling (reduce downtime)
  • Glass or ceramic cartridge fuses – common in older or simpler systems
  • HRC fuses (High-Rupturing Capacity) – used for higher currents; contain silica sand to quench the arc

3️⃣ Slow-Blow vs Fast-Acting Fuses

Slow-Blow Fuses

Designed to tolerate a brief increase in current without immediately blowing. Useful for equipment that draws a high starting current (motors, transformers, some X-ray components).

🧠 Analogy

A security guard who sees someone running quickly does not immediately assume danger. The guard waits to see if the unusual activity continues. Brief surge → wait. Persistent overcurrent → disconnect.

Fast-Acting Fuses

Respond very quickly to dangerous overcurrent. Essential for protecting sensitive components such as diodes, transistors and integrated circuits.

HRC fuses use silica sand as an arc-quenching medium.

4️⃣ Fuse Components & Ratings

Main parts

  • Fuse element – the sacrificial part that melts
  • Fuse link (or fuse block) – houses the element (glass or ceramic)
  • Fuse powder – helps prevent arcing (chalk, dust, POP, ground ceramic)
  • Fuse cap

Ratings

Rating Meaning
Current rating Maximum current the fuse can carry without blowing
Voltage rating Maximum voltage it can safely interrupt without dangerous arcing

Using a fuse with too low a rating causes unnecessary interruptions.
Using a fuse with too high a rating fails to protect the circuit.

5️⃣ Circuit Breakers

A circuit breaker is a reusable protective device. It interrupts current during overload or fault and can afterwards be reset.

Feature Fuse Circuit Breaker
Action Melts Trips / opens
After fault Usually needs replacement Can be reset
Nature Sacrificial Reusable
🧠 Analogy

A fuse is a breakable emergency link — once it breaks you replace it.
A circuit breaker is an automatic door that closes during danger and can later be opened/reset.

6️⃣ Thermal Circuit Breakers

Mainly used for low-voltage sections. They use a bimetallic strip (two different metals joined together).

When heated, the two metals expand differently, so the strip bends.

High current → Heating → Bimetallic strip bends → Spring mechanism operates → Switch opens → Circuit interrupted

After tripping, some thermal breakers cannot be reset until the strip has cooled.

7️⃣ Electromagnetic Circuit Breakers

Often used for high-voltage circuits. They rely on electromagnetism rather than heat.

High current → Electromagnet becomes strongly magnetized → Armature is attracted → Spring-loaded switch trips → Circuit opens

Advantage: can usually be reset immediately without waiting for cooling. Many have a reset button.

8️⃣ Switches

A switch makes or breaks an electrical circuit (controls whether the circuit is closed or open).

Mains Supply Switch (Knife Switch)

Usually a knife-type switch mounted on the wall near the generator or control panel. Isolates the X-ray unit from the mains supply.

  • Consists of a heavy metal moving part, insulated handle and metallic contacts
  • May be one-, two-, three- or four-pole

Limitation: Switching speed depends on the operator. Slow movement can cause arcing, so knife switches are not used for high-voltage switching.

Magnetic Relays

Use electromagnetism to perform switching. Contain two circuits:

  • Magnetic / control circuit
  • Switch circuit

A small control signal can operate a larger power circuit.

Type Default State When Energized
Normally Closed (NC) Contacts closed Contacts open
Normally Open (NO) Contacts open Contacts close

⭐ High-Yield Points

  • Fuse must be connected in series
  • Slow-blow → tolerates brief startup surges
  • Fast-acting → protects sensitive semiconductor circuits
  • HRC fuse uses silica sand to quench the arc
  • Current rating = max continuous current
  • Voltage rating = max safe interrupting voltage
  • Thermal breaker: Heat → Bend → Trip
  • Electromagnetic breaker: Magnet → Pull → Trip
  • Circuit breakers are reusable; fuses are usually not
  • Knife switch = manual mains isolation (not for high voltage)
  • Magnetic relay: small control circuit operates larger switch circuit
  • NC = closed when no power; NO = open when no power
