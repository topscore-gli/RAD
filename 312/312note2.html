RAD 312

⚡ The Mains Circuit

Power Entry • Switches • Voltage Stabilization • Uniform X-ray Beam

📋 One-Sentence Summary

The mains circuit creates a stable potential difference between the cathode and anode of the X-ray tube so that electrons can be accelerated uniformly to produce a consistent X-ray beam.

🧠 Overall Flow

Mains supply → Mains switch → Line voltage compensator → Mains X-ray circuit → Cathode–anode potential difference → X-ray production

1️⃣ The Mains Circuit

The mains X-ray circuit is a circuit that is separate from the filament circuit.

Main function: To create a potential difference (voltage) between the cathode and anode of the X-ray tube.

This potential difference is important because it provides the electrical force needed for electrons to move from the cathode toward the anode during X-ray production.

Why must the voltage be constant?

The material emphasizes that the potential difference should remain constant throughout X-ray production. This is necessary to produce a uniform X-ray beam.

🧠 Simple Analogy

Imagine water flowing through a pipe. If the water pressure keeps changing, the amount of water coming out will fluctuate. Similarly, if the voltage supplied to the X-ray tube fluctuates, the X-ray output can become inconsistent.
Stable voltage → stable electron acceleration → more uniform X-ray production.

The components of the mains circuit are discussed beginning from the mains supply and moving toward the X-ray tube.

2️⃣ The Mains Supply

The mains supply is the electrical source that provides power to the X-ray machine.

According to the material, the supply can come from:

A. Wall socket — 240 V

A wall socket can provide approximately 240 volts. The material associates this supply with low-powered X-ray units, particularly mobile X-ray units and portable X-ray units.

B. Combined line voltages — 415 V

For high-powered X-ray units, the mains switch can combine two line voltages of 240 V to provide approximately 415 V.

Type of X-ray unit Supply mentioned
Low-powered / mobile / portable 240 V
High-powered 415 V
🧠 Easy Analogy

Think of electricity like fuel for a vehicle.
A small vehicle may need less power.
A large vehicle requires a greater power supply.
Similarly, lower-powered X-ray equipment can use the lower supply described in the material, while high-powered equipment requires a higher supply.

3️⃣ The Mains Switch

The mains switch controls the electrical supply entering the X-ray system. The material identifies six possible types of switches that may be encountered in an X-ray room:

3.1 Toggle Switch

A toggle switch is a simple mechanical switch operated by moving a lever. Moving the lever can complete the circuit (electricity flows) or break the circuit (electricity stops). In X-ray machines, toggle switches may be found particularly in smaller or older systems for turning the machine ON or OFF.

  • Advantages: Simple, reliable, easy to operate
  • Limitation: It is mainly a manual control and is not well suited to advanced systems requiring remote control, automation or advanced safety features
🧠 Analogy

A toggle switch is like a traditional light switch on a wall: you physically move it to turn something on or off.

3.2 Push-Button Switch

A push-button switch operates when a button is pressed. The material identifies two forms:

  • Momentary push-button – The circuit is completed only while the button is being pressed. Once you release it, the circuit returns to its previous state.
  • Latching push-button – The switch remains in its ON or OFF condition until it is pressed again.

Push-button switches may be used for emergency shut-offs on X-ray machines.

  • Main advantage: They allow rapid, one-touch operation
  • Limitation: They are more appropriate for quick operations rather than continuous manual control of power
🧠 Analogy

A doorbell is a good example of a momentary switch: pressing it activates it, while releasing it stops the action. A push-to-turn-on/push-to-turn-off appliance button is closer to a latching switch.

3.3 Knife Switch

A knife switch is a large manual switch. It contains a lever or blade that is physically moved to connect the circuit or disconnect the circuit. The material states that knife switches are designed to handle high currents. They are typically associated with heavy-duty systems and older X-ray machines.

🧠 Analogy

Imagine a physical metal bridge that connects two roads. Bridge connected → current can pass. Bridge lifted → current cannot pass. The knife switch works on a similar physical principle.

3.4 Circuit Breaker Switch

A circuit breaker performs two functions:

  • Function 1 — Switching: It can be used to open or close the electrical circuit.
  • Function 2 — Protection: It automatically opens the circuit when it detects conditions such as overload or short circuit.

This protects the X-ray machine, the electrical system, and the operator/equipment from consequences of electrical faults. After tripping, the circuit breaker can generally be manually reset. The material describes circuit breakers as commonly used in modern X-ray machines because of their protective function.

  • Advantages: Provides protection, can automatically interrupt dangerous electrical conditions, can be reset, suitable for systems requiring high protection
  • Limitation: Compared with simpler switches, they can be bulkier and more expensive
🧠 Analogy

Think of a circuit breaker as an automatic security guard. A normal switch simply follows your command (“ON” → ON, “OFF” → OFF). A circuit breaker can say: “Something is wrong with the electrical current, so I’m shutting the system down.”

3.5 Magnetic Contactor

A magnetic contactor is an electrically operated switch. Instead of someone physically moving the switch, an electrical signal creates a magnetic field, which causes the contactor to connect or disconnect the power circuit. It is widely used in industrial systems, medical equipment and X-ray machines. It is particularly useful when remote or automatic control is required.

Characteristics: Magnetic contactors can handle high voltage, handle high current, support automation, allow remote control and be used in large systems.

  • Limitation: Compared with simple manual switches such as toggles, they are more complex and more expensive
🧠 Analogy

Imagine a very heavy door. Instead of a person physically opening the door, you press a small button and a motor mechanism opens the heavy door. The button controls the mechanism, while the mechanism handles the heavier job. Similarly, a relatively small electrical control signal can operate a contactor that controls a larger electrical power circuit.

3.6 Remote-Controlled Switch

A remote-controlled switch allows an operator to control the electrical switch from a distance. The control may be through wireless systems, a control panel or a computer interface. These switches can be found in advanced medical equipment, including high-end X-ray systems.

Why use remote control? It allows remote operation, automation, greater flexibility, integration with other systems and improved convenience.

  • Limitation: Remote-controlled systems are more expensive and require specialized installation. Therefore they are more suitable for complex or high-technology systems.
🧠 Analogy

A television remote control is a simple everyday example. You don’t need to physically touch the television’s power switch. You send a command from a distance. A remote-controlled X-ray switch applies the same basic concept to electrical equipment, although the actual system is much more sophisticated.

4️⃣ Circuit Breaker vs Magnetic Contactor vs Remote Switch

These three can be confusing, so remember their main purposes:

Component Main idea
Circuit breaker Switching + electrical protection
Magnetic contactor Electrically controlled switching of high power
Remote-controlled switch Allows switching from a distance
🧠 Easy Memory Trick

Breaker = Protect
Contactor = Control
Remote switch = Distance

5️⃣ Line Voltage Compensators

The final component introduced in this material is the line voltage compensator. These are located between the mains supply and the mains circuit. Their purpose is to stabilize fluctuations in voltage coming from the mains supply.

Why is this important?

The X-ray tube requires a stable potential difference during X-ray production. If the incoming electrical voltage changes significantly, the voltage supplied to the X-ray system can also fluctuate. The line voltage compensator therefore helps maintain a more stable electrical supply.

🧠 Analogy

Imagine a water tank supplying water to a machine. If the pressure from the city water supply constantly changes (high pressure → low pressure → high pressure → low pressure), the machine may not operate consistently. A regulator can help maintain a more stable pressure. A line voltage compensator performs a similar stabilizing role for electrical voltage.

🎯 Exam-Focused Understanding

  • If asked “What is the function of the mains X-ray circuit?”: It creates the potential difference between the cathode and anode of the X-ray tube and maintains the electrical conditions necessary for uniform X-ray production.
  • If asked “What is the function of a circuit breaker?”: It acts as both a switch and a protective device, automatically opening the circuit during an overload or short circuit.
  • If asked “What is the function of a line voltage compensator?”: It helps stabilize fluctuations in the voltage supplied from the mains.
  • If asked to differentiate the switches, remember:
    Toggle → manual lever
    Push-button → press
    Knife → physical blade/lever
    Circuit breaker → protection
    Magnetic contactor → electromagnetic control
    Remote-controlled → distance control
