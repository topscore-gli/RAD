RAD 312

⚡ The Mains Circuit

Power Entry • Switches • Voltage Stabilization • Uniform X-ray Beam

📋 One-Sentence Summary

The mains circuit creates a stable potential difference between the cathode and anode of the X-ray tube so that electrons can be accelerated uniformly to produce a consistent X-ray beam.

🧠 Overall Flow

Mains supply → Mains switch → Line voltage compensator → Mains circuit → Cathode–anode potential difference → X-ray production

1️⃣ The Mains Circuit

The mains circuit is completely separate from the filament circuit.

Main function: To create a potential difference (voltage) between the cathode and anode of the X-ray tube.

This potential difference provides the electrical force that drives electrons from the cathode toward the anode during X-ray production.

Why must the voltage be constant?

A constant potential difference is required to produce a uniform X-ray beam.

🧠 Analogy

Imagine water flowing through a pipe. If the water pressure keeps changing, the amount of water coming out will fluctuate. In the same way, if the voltage supplied to the X-ray tube fluctuates, the X-ray output becomes inconsistent.

Stable voltage → stable electron acceleration → more uniform X-ray production.

2️⃣ The Mains Supply

The mains supply is the electrical source that powers the X-ray machine.

Type of X-ray Unit Supply Voltage
Low-powered / Mobile / Portable ≈ 240 V (wall socket)
High-powered units ≈ 415 V (combined line voltages)
🧠 Analogy

Think of electricity like fuel for a vehicle.
A small vehicle needs less power.
A large vehicle requires a greater power supply.
The same principle applies to low-powered versus high-powered X-ray equipment.

3️⃣ Types of Mains Switches

Six possible types of switches may be found in an X-ray room:

3.1 Toggle Switch

A simple mechanical switch operated by moving a lever.

  • Moving the lever completes the circuit → electricity flows
  • Moving it the other way breaks the circuit → electricity stops

Common in smaller or older systems for basic ON/OFF control.

Advantage: Simple, reliable, easy to operate
Limitation: Manual only – not ideal for remote control or automation

🧠 Analogy

Just like a traditional wall light switch – you physically flip it to turn something on or off.

3.2 Push-Button Switch

Operated by pressing a button. Two forms exist:

  • Momentary – Circuit is closed only while the button is held down
  • Latching – Stays ON or OFF until pressed again

Often used for emergency shut-offs.

Advantage: Rapid one-touch operation
Limitation: Better for quick actions than continuous manual control

🧠 Analogy

A doorbell is a momentary switch. A push-to-start appliance button is closer to a latching switch.

3.3 Knife Switch

A large manual switch with a lever (blade) that is physically moved to connect or disconnect the circuit.

Designed to handle high currents. Common in heavy-duty or older X-ray machines.

🧠 Analogy

Imagine a physical metal bridge connecting two roads.
Bridge down → current can pass
Bridge lifted → current cannot pass

3.4 Circuit Breaker Switch

Performs two important jobs:

  • Switching – Can open or close the circuit
  • Protection – Automatically opens the circuit during overload or short circuit

Protects the machine, the electrical system, and the operator. Can usually be reset manually after tripping.

Advantage: Provides automatic protection and can be reset
Limitation: Bulkier and more expensive than simple switches

🧠 Analogy

A normal switch only does what you tell it.
A circuit breaker is like an automatic security guard that shuts the system down when something is wrong.

3.5 Magnetic Contactor

An electrically operated switch. A small control signal creates a magnetic field that opens or closes the main power contacts.

Widely used when remote or automatic control of high power is needed.

Advantage: Handles high voltage/current, supports automation and remote control
Limitation: More complex and expensive than simple manual switches

🧠 Analogy

Like a very heavy door. Instead of pushing the heavy door yourself, you press a small button and a motor opens it for you. A small control signal operates a contactor that controls a much larger power circuit.

3.6 Remote-Controlled Switch

Allows the operator to control the switch from a distance (wireless, control panel, or computer interface).

Found in advanced high-end X-ray systems.

Advantage: Remote operation, automation, greater flexibility
Limitation: More expensive and requires specialized installation

🧠 Analogy

Just like a television remote – you control the power from a distance without physically touching the equipment.

4️⃣ Quick Comparison of Key Switches

Component Main Idea
Circuit Breaker Switching + electrical protection
Magnetic Contactor Electrically controlled switching of high power
Remote-controlled Switch Allows switching from a distance
🧠 Easy Memory Trick

Breaker = Protect
Contactor = Control
Remote switch = Distance

5️⃣ Line Voltage Compensator

Located between the mains supply and the mains circuit.

Purpose: To stabilize fluctuations in the voltage coming from the mains supply.

The X-ray tube needs a stable potential difference. If the incoming voltage keeps changing, the voltage supplied to the tube will also fluctuate, affecting X-ray quality.

🧠 Analogy

Imagine a water tank supplying a machine. If the city water pressure constantly changes (high → low → high), the machine will not run consistently. A pressure regulator keeps the pressure stable.
A line voltage compensator does the same job for electrical voltage.

⭐ High-Yield Points

  • Mains circuit is separate from the filament circuit
  • Main job: create potential difference between cathode and anode
  • Voltage must stay constant → uniform X-ray beam
  • 240 V → low-powered / mobile / portable units
  • 415 V → high-powered units
  • Toggle = simple manual lever
  • Push-button = press (momentary or latching)
  • Knife = large physical blade for high current
  • Circuit breaker = switching + automatic protection
  • Magnetic contactor = electrically controlled high-power switching
  • Remote-controlled = operate from a distance
  • Line voltage compensator = stabilizes incoming voltage fluctuations
