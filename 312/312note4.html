RAD 312

⚡ High-Tension Cables, Plugs & Sockets

Safe High-Voltage Transmission • Cable Construction • Earth Continuity

📋 One-Sentence Summary

High-tension cables safely carry the extremely high voltage from the generator to the X-ray tube while preventing leakage, minimising voltage loss, and protecting people through multiple layers of insulation and an earthed metal screen.

🧠 Layer Memory Trick

C-I-E-O
Central conductor → Inner insulation → Earth braid → Outer cover

1️⃣ Why Are Special Cables Needed?

X-ray equipment uses very high electrical voltages. Ordinary wires cannot safely connect the high-voltage generator to the X-ray tube.

Important safety point:
Exposure to the high voltage in an X-ray system can be fatal and can cause severe tissue burns.

Therefore the high-tension cable has two major jobs:

  • Carry the high voltage safely
  • Prevent the high voltage from escaping and injuring people
🧠 Analogy

High-tension electricity is like very hot water under high pressure inside a pipe.
You would never use a thin drinking straw — it would fail.
You need a specially designed pipe that can withstand the pressure, does not leak, and does not allow the contents to escape.

2️⃣ Main Requirements of High-Tension Cables

Manufacturers design the cables to satisfy four major requirements:

  1. Withstand high voltage – insulation must not break down
  2. Prevent external leakage – protect radiographer, patient and staff
  3. Minimise voltage loss – so the correct voltage reaches the tube
  4. Be reasonably affordable – balance safety, performance, durability and cost

3️⃣ Types According to Equipment Size

Type Used With
Heavy-duty cables Three-phase generators and large systems
Low-duty cables Mobile, portable and low-powered units
🧠 Analogy

Large truck → heavy-duty road equipment
Small car → lighter-duty equipment
Same principle applies to X-ray systems and their cables.

4️⃣ Function of the High-Tension Cable

The high-tension cable connects:

High-tension generator → High-tension cable → X-ray tube

It must transmit the very high voltage safely and withstand heat, mechanical stress and high electrical stress.

High-tension plugs and sockets at each end allow connection to the generator and the X-ray tube.

5️⃣ Construction of the High-Tension Cable

A high-tension cable is not a simple wire. It has several carefully designed layers.

1. Central Conductor

Carries the electrical supply. Made of several copper wires surrounded by high-grade plastic insulator.

  • Copper is used because it conducts electricity well
  • Several thinner wires are used instead of one thick wire to reduce heat and improve flexibility

2. Anode vs Cathode Cables (Traditional)

Section Number of Cores
Anode One central core
Cathode Three central cores
(broad focus, fine focus, common connection)

Modern cables are commonly made with three cores for cost reduction and easier replacement.

3. Inner Insulating Covering

A thick layer of insulation around the central cores. Provides additional electrical protection.

Thicker insulation → bulkier, more expensive and less flexible cable

4. Earth Cable Braid

A metallic sheath made of woven wire strands. Connected to earth. Forms an earthed metal screen around the insulated conductors.

5. Outer Cable Cover

The outermost layer (usually plastic). Protects the flexible earth braid underneath.

🧠 Layer Order (Inside → Outside)

Central cores → Inner insulation → Earth cable braid → Outer cover
Remember: C-I-E-O

6️⃣ Cable Connection, Plugs & Sockets

Three Pins

The socket has three pins that correspond to the three central conductors.

Orientation Groove

An outer acceptor groove ensures the cable can only be inserted in the correct orientation (similar to a keyed USB connector).

Snap Ring

Covers the bore into which the cable socket fits.

Gland Nut

An overhanging metal screw cap that secures the cable and socket firmly in position at the X-ray tube.

🧠 Analogy

The gland nut acts like a locking collar that mechanically secures the connection.

Continuous Earth Screen

The body of the high-tension plug is connected to the earth cable braid. This maintains a continuous earthed metal screen that is continuous with:

  • the body of the X-ray tube
  • the high-tension generator

⭐ High-Yield Points

  • Special cables are needed because of extremely high voltages (can be fatal)
  • Main jobs: carry high voltage safely + prevent leakage
  • Four requirements: withstand voltage, prevent leakage, minimise loss, be affordable
  • Heavy-duty cables → large systems; Low-duty → mobile/portable
  • Central conductor = copper wires
  • Traditional: Anode = 1 core, Cathode = 3 cores
  • Modern cables usually have 3 cores
  • Earth cable braid = earthed metallic screen
  • Outer cover protects the earth braid
  • Gland nut secures the cable/socket connection
  • Continuous earth screen links tube and generator
  • Thicker insulation = bulkier, costlier, less flexible cable

🧠 Final Mental Model

Think of the high-tension cable as a high-pressure water hose with safety layers:
Copper core = water-carrying pipe
Insulation = thick wall preventing leakage
Earth braid = protective metal shield
Outer cover = tough outer jacket
Plug/socket = secure connector at each end
