RAD 312

⚡ General Introduction to Equipment Engineering

X-ray Circuit • Power Supply • Filament Circuit • Rectification • High-yield Summary

📋 One-Sentence Summary

An X-ray machine converts electrical energy into X-rays by heating the cathode filament to release electrons, accelerating those electrons across a high-voltage potential toward the anode, and controlling the process with appropriate voltage, current, rectification, and exposure timing.

🧠 Memory Map

POWER → CONTROL → HEAT → RELEASE → ACCELERATE → COLLIDE → X-RAY

1️⃣ What is Equipment Engineering?

The main aim is to understand the components that make X-ray production possible.

Basic principle: Fast-moving electrons strike a target → X-rays are produced.

Two major types of X-radiation can be produced:

  • Bremsstrahlung radiation
  • Characteristic radiation

Although the idea sounds simple, an X-ray machine needs a carefully controlled environment. Several electrical and mechanical factors must remain stable for useful and safe X-ray production.

Basic Sequence

Power supply → Controls → X-ray tube → X-rays

🧠 Easy Analogy

Think of an X-ray machine like a water supply system:
• Power supply = water source/pump
• Control panel = taps and valves
• X-ray tube = the place where the process occurs
• X-rays = the final product

2️⃣ Major Visible Components of an X-ray Room

Visible components

  • X-ray tube and housing – Usually positioned above the examination couch. This is where X-rays are generated.
  • X-ray examination couch – Where the patient is positioned.
  • Control panel – Where the radiographer selects exposure factors (kVp, mA, time).

Hidden components

  • High-tension generator
  • High-tension cables

The purpose of studying the equipment is to follow the journey of electrical current and voltage from the power source until X-rays are finally produced inside the tube.

3️⃣ The X-ray Circuit

The X-ray circuit converts electrical energy → X-ray energy.

The mains supply normally provides alternating current (AC), but the X-ray equipment requires suitable direct current (DC).

Two major types of power supply

1. Linear Power Supply
Uses transformer + rectifier + filter to convert AC into suitable DC.

2. Switch-Mode Power Supply (SMPS)
Uses high-frequency switching to achieve the required conversion. It can take power from AC mains or DC sources.

4️⃣ How SMPS Works (4 Stages)

Stage 1: Input Rectification

If the input is AC, it is first converted into DC using a rectifier (AC → Rectifier → DC). If the input is already DC, this stage is skipped.

Stage 2: High-frequency Switching

A high-frequency oscillator controls transistors that switch ON and OFF rapidly. This controls the flow of current to the transformer.

🧠 Analogy

Think of a very fast electrical gate that repeatedly opens and closes.

Stage 3: Transformation and Rectification

The switched current passes through a transformer (which can step voltage up or down). After transformation, it is rectified again to produce DC.

Stage 4: Filtering and Output Regulation

Capacitors and filters smooth the voltage, reducing noise, fluctuations and ripple. The output is regulated so the required voltage remains stable.

5️⃣ Advantages & Limitations of SMPS

Advantages

  • High efficiency – Operates at high frequencies (tens to hundreds of kHz)
  • Small and lightweight – Smaller transformers can be used
  • Wide input voltage range – Works with 110 V or 230 V systems
  • Better heat management – Less energy wasted as heat
  • Stable output voltage – Critical for consistent X-ray production
🧠 Analogy

If an oven temperature keeps fluctuating (180°C → 150°C → 200°C), the cakes will not come out consistently. A regulated SMPS keeps the “oven temperature” stable for X-ray production.

Limitations

  • Electromagnetic interference
  • More complex design
  • Some residual ripple and noise

6️⃣ Major Components of the X-ray Circuit

1. Power Supply / High-voltage Generator

Provides the high voltage required by the X-ray tube (typical range: 40 kV – 150 kV). It converts ordinary mains voltage (240 V or 415 V) into the much higher voltage needed.

2. Control Panel

The radiographer selects:

  • kVp
  • mA
  • Exposure time

7️⃣ Filament Circuit & X-ray Tube

Filament Circuit

Supplies low voltage to the filament inside the cathode. Its purpose is to heat the filament so that electrons are released by thermionic emission.

🧠 Analogy

Think of an electric stove. When current passes through the heating element, electrical energy turns into heat. When the filament is hot enough, electrons are released.

X-ray Tube

The core component where X-rays are produced. It contains two major electrodes:

  • Cathode – Contains the filament. Produces and releases electrons.
  • Anode – Serves as the target. Electrons strike the anode to produce X-rays.

Basic process:
Heated cathode → electrons released → high voltage accelerates electrons → electrons hit anode → X-rays produced

High-tension Cables

Transmit the high voltage between the high-tension generator and the X-ray tube. Because the voltage is extremely high, the cables must be properly insulated.

8️⃣ Two Major Circuits

Circuit Main Role
Mains Circuit Controls the potential difference between cathode and anode (accelerates electrons)
Filament Circuit Controls heating of the filament (determines number of electrons available)

Mains Circuit Sections

  • Primary / Low-voltage section
  • Secondary / High-voltage section

These two sections are separated by the high-tension step-up transformer.

9️⃣ Journey of Electricity Through the Circuit

Step 1: Power enters

Electrical power comes from the mains supply and passes through the mains switch.

Step 2: Circuit breaker

Protects the equipment. If excessive voltage or current is detected, it opens the circuit (like a security guard).

Step 3: Line voltage compensator

Corrects variations in the incoming mains voltage.

Step 4: Autotransformer

Uses self-induction to adjust the voltage supplied to the primary winding of the high-tension transformer. Controlled by the kVp selector (major + minor) and monitored by the kVp meter.

Step 5: Exposure & Timing controls

The exposure control closes the circuit. The timer determines how long the circuit stays closed (how long X-rays are produced).

🧠 Analogy

Opening a tap = starting X-ray production
Keeping it open = duration of exposure
Closing it = stopping X-ray production

Step 6: Step-up transformer & Rectifiers

Voltage is increased by the high-tension step-up transformer, then passed through rectifiers before reaching the X-ray tube.

🔟 Filament Circuit Components

The filament circuit starts from the autotransformer and includes:

  • Filament stabilizer – Corrects line voltage variations
  • mA selector – Determines current to the filament (approx. 3–5 A at 6–10 V)
  • Filament ammeter – Measures filament current
  • Space-charge compensator – Maintains filament current as kVp changes
  • Step-down transformer – Reduces voltage and increases current for the filament
  • Focal spot selector – Selects which filament/focal spot is used

1️⃣1️⃣ Stationary vs Rotating Anode Tubes

Type Key Features
Stationary anode Low-power units. One-step exposure system.
Rotating anode Higher power handling. Two-step exposure: Prepare → Expose

Prepare Stage (Rotating Anode)

  • Rotating anode is energized and reaches maximum speed
  • Filament circuit is closed and the filament is heated
  • Thermionic emission begins
🧠 Analogy

Like starting a car: start the engine and allow it to reach operating condition before driving. Prepare → anode spins + filament heats → then Expose.

1️⃣2️⃣ Why Rectification is Necessary

Alternating current has a sinusoidal waveform that alternates between positive half-cycle, zero, and negative half-cycle. The direction of voltage therefore changes periodically.

The X-ray tube needs the polarity to remain correct at all times:

Cathode = negative → Anode = positive
So electrons always travel from cathode to anode.

What happens without rectification?

  • During the negative half-cycle the polarity reverses
  • Cathode becomes positive and anode becomes negative
  • Electrons could travel in the wrong direction
  • This can damage the filament and stop useful X-ray production

Rectifiers ensure the voltage/current stays in the required direction so electrons always move correctly from filament to anode.

1️⃣3️⃣ Complete Sequence of X-ray Production

  1. Filament is heated
  2. Thermionic emission releases electrons
  3. High voltage creates potential difference between cathode and anode
  4. Electrons are accelerated across the tube
  5. Electrons strike the anode/target
  6. X-rays are produced
  7. Process continues until the selected exposure time ends
  8. Timer opens the circuit
  9. X-ray production stops
