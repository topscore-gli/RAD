SERIES 1: Electromagnetic Radiation & The Spectrum

What is EMR? Transfer of energy through space by combined electric and magnetic fields. It has dual nature (wave and particle).

Wave Evidence

  • Interference (Fresnel)
  • Diffraction

Particle Evidence

  • Compton effect
  • Photoelectric emission

Important Relations

  • c = ν × λ
  • E = hν = hc/λ
  • c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s
  • hc ≈ 12.4 keV·Å or 1240 eV·nm

Electromagnetic Spectrum (long → short wavelength)

  • Radio / TV / Microwaves → Communication, Telemedicine
  • Infrared → Thermography, heat detection
  • Visible light → Image formation on screens & film
  • Ultraviolet → Sterilization, Vitamin D, phototherapy
  • X-rays & Gamma rays → Imaging and therapy
Q1. Calculate the energy of an infrared photon with λ = 1000 nm.
E = 1240 / 1000 = 1.24 eV
Q2. Calculate the energy of UV light with λ = 250 nm.
E = 1240 / 250 = 4.96 eV
Q3. Differentiate between X-rays and Gamma rays.
X-rays → produced by electron transitions or Bremsstrahlung.
Gamma rays → produced by nuclear transitions.
Origin is different even if energy is the same.

SERIES 2: Atomic Structure & Quantum Mechanics

Bohr’s Model (1913)

  • Electrons move in fixed permitted orbits
  • No radiation while in permitted orbit
  • Radiation occurs only when electron jumps between orbits

de Broglie Hypothesis

Every particle has an associated wavelength: λ = h / p

Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle

Δx · Δp ≥ ħ / 4π (cannot know position and momentum exactly at the same time)

Four Quantum Numbers

  • n – Principal (shell: K, L, M…)
  • l – Azimuthal (s, p, d, f)
  • ml – Magnetic (–l to +l)
  • ms – Spin (+½ or –½)
Q4. Write the electron configuration of Magnesium (Z = 12) and its valency electrons.

Valency electrons = 3s²
Q5. Write the electron configuration of Manganese (Z = 25).
1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s² 3d⁵

SERIES 3: X-ray Discovery, Production & Factors

Discovery: Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen, 8 November 1895, Würzburg, Germany. First Nobel Prize in Physics (1901).

Two Production Mechanisms

  • Bremsstrahlung – continuous spectrum (electron decelerated by nucleus)
  • Characteristic radiation – line spectrum (inner-shell electron ejected)

Important formula: λmin = 12.4 / kVp (Å)

Factors Affecting the X-ray Beam

  • kVp → Quality + Quantity
  • mA / mAs → Quantity only
  • Filtration → Increases mean energy, reduces intensity
  • Target Z → Higher Z = more efficient production
  • Waveform → 3-phase / HF better than single-phase
Q6. An X-ray tube operates at 80 kVp. Calculate λmin.
λmin = 12.4 / 80 = 0.155 Å
Q7. What is the maximum photon energy at 120 kVp?
120 keV
Q8. List factors that affect Quality and factors that affect Quantity.
Quality: kVp, Filtration, Target Z, Waveform
Quantity: mA/mAs, kVp, Filtration, Target Z, Waveform, Distance

SERIES 4: Radioactivity & Nuclear Models

Discovery: Becquerel (uranium), Marie & Pierre Curie (coined “radioactivity”, discovered Po & Ra).

Nuclear Models

  • Rutherford – nucleus contains protons & neutrons
  • Liquid Drop Model
  • Shell Model (Magic numbers: 2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126)

Types of Decay

  • Alpha (α) – emits ⁴₂He (A↓4, Z↓2)
  • Beta-minus (β⁻) – neutron → proton (Z↑1)
  • Beta-plus (β⁺) – proton → neutron (Z↓1)
  • Gamma (γ) – isomeric transition (no change in A or Z)
  • Electron Capture & Internal Conversion

Important Equations

  • Nt = N₀ e–λt
  • T½ = 0.693 / λ
  • 1/Teff = 1/Tphysical + 1/Tbiological
Q9. Write the nuclear equation for α-decay of Uranium-238.
²³⁸₉₂U → ²³⁴₉₀Th + ⁴₂He
Q10. Write the nuclear equation for β⁻ decay of Carbon-14.
¹⁴₆C → ¹⁴₇N + β⁻ + antineutrino
Q11. λ = 0.1155 day⁻¹. Calculate the half-life.
T½ = 0.693 / 0.1155 ≈ 6 days
Q12. Initial activity = 800 MBq, T½ = 6 h. Activity after 18 hours?
3 half-lives → 800 / 8 = 100 MBq
Q13. Physical T½ = 6 h, Biological T½ = 10 h. Find effective half-life.
1/Teff = 1/6 + 1/10 = 0.2667 → Teff ≈ 3.75 hours
