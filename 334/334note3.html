2️⃣ General Preparation and Safety

  • Confirm: patient identity, request, side and clinical indication. Ask about pain, injury mechanism, surgery and ability to move the limb.
  • Explain and prepare: describe the procedure; remove watches, rings, jewellery and clothing artefacts if safe. Maintain privacy and dignity.
  • Trauma: never force a painful or deformed limb into a textbook position. Support the limb, seek assistance and adapt views to the trauma protocol.
  • Radiation safety: follow local pregnancy-screening and justification policy; collimate carefully and avoid repeats.
  • Marker: use the correct R/L marker before exposure, positioned so it does not obscure anatomy.
  • Two projections: most routine appendicular examinations need views approximately at right angles. Include the region of interest and the required adjacent joint(s).
  • Exposure: course values are educational figures, not universal prescriptions. Use the approved departmental chart in practice.

3️⃣ Hand and Fingers

Course Views and Centring

  • Dorsi-palmar (DP): CP at head of 3rd metacarpal. Position palm flat, fingers extended and slightly separated, without rotation.
  • DP/anterior oblique: course CP at head of 5th metacarpal; CR angled to pass through head of 3rd metacarpal. Commonly taught oblique rotation is about 45°.
  • Lateral hand: supplementary; CP at head of 2nd metacarpal. A fan lateral can separate digits where required.
  • Both-hands DP: course CP midway between interphalangeal joints of both thumbs.
  • Ball-catcher/Nørgaard: course CP midway between both hands at level of 5th metacarpals.

Collimation & Image Evaluation (Supplemental)

  • Include all required digits, metacarpals and soft tissues; for a hand view include the full hand and relevant wrist-side metacarpal ends.
  • DP: fingers separated, no avoidable rotation, joints and soft tissues adequately demonstrated.
  • Oblique: check the degree of rotation and overlap against the view requested.
  • For a single digit, centre to the affected digit’s PIP joint as a common reference, and include the full digit plus adjacent anatomy according to protocol.

Course factors: 50–55 kVp, 2–3 mAs. Cassette: 10 × 8 inches listed.

4️⃣ Wrist + Dedicated Scaphoid Series

Course Centring and Views

  • PA wrist: midway between radial and ulnar styloid processes.
  • Lateral wrist: styloid process of radius.
  • Anterior oblique: supplementary; commonly rotates the wrist about 45°.

Positioning, Collimation & Image Evaluation (Supplemental)

  • PA: seat patient beside table, shoulder/elbow/wrist in the same plane, forearm pronated and palm down.
  • Lateral: turn wrist/forearm into a true lateral with thumb side up; avoid hand tilt.
  • Collimate to distal radius/ulna, carpals and proximal metacarpals.
  • Check coverage, motion, rotation and visibility of carpal alignment.

Course factors: 50–55 kVp, 2.5–4 mAs. Cassette: 10 × 8 inches listed.

🦴 Dedicated Scaphoid Series (Supplemental – Essential for Viva)

The source mentions “suspected scaphoid injury may need dedicated views per protocol” but does not detail them. These are the standard views examiners expect:

  • PA wrist with ulnar deviation (elongates the scaphoid)
  • PA oblique (45°)
  • Lateral wrist
  • Stecher view (PA with 20° cephalic angle or clenched-fist ulnar deviation)
  • Some departments add a 30° elongated view or carpal-tunnel view

Surrounding bones you must name:

  • Proximal row: Scaphoid, Lunate, Triquetrum, Pisiform
  • Distal row: Trapezium, Trapezoid, Capitate, Hamate
  • Also: distal radius, distal ulna, proximal metacarpals
Memory tip for carpals

“Some Lovers Try Positions That They Can’t Handle” → Scaphoid, Lunate, Triquetrum, Pisiform, Trapezium, Trapezoid, Capitate, Hamate.

Image criteria for scaphoid series: entire scaphoid demonstrated without foreshortening; no overlap of adjacent carpals on elongated views; soft-tissue margins included; correct marker and no motion.

5️⃣ Forearm

  • AP: CP midway between elbow and wrist joints. Lateral: listed as the second basic view.
  • AP positioning: extend elbow as tolerated, supinate hand and align shoulder, elbow and wrist in one plane.
  • Lateral positioning: flex elbow about 90° if safe and turn thumb side upward; do not force painful movement.
  • Critical coverage: include both elbow and wrist joints on each projection.
  • Collimate to the full forearm with soft-tissue margins. Check both joints, rotation, marker and motion.

Course factors: 60–65 kVp, 5–6 mAs. Cassette-size alignment unclear in extracted table — verify original.

6️⃣ Elbow

Course Centring and Views

  • Lateral: lateral epicondyle of humerus.
  • AP: 2.5 cm distal to and midway between the medial and lateral epicondyles of the humerus.
  • Supplementary views named: AP partial flexion; AP forearm-in-contact; AP upper-arm-in-contact; AP full flexion/axial projections; oblique proximal radioulnar joint; axial ulnar groove.

Positioning & Image Evaluation (Supplemental)

  • AP: extend elbow as tolerated, supinate hand and align epicondyles parallel to receptor.
  • Lateral: flex elbow about 90° if possible, thumb up and epicondyles superimposed.
  • If extension is impossible, do not force it; partial-flexion projections may be used under protocol.
  • Collimate to distal humerus, elbow joint and proximal radius/ulna.

Course factors: 60–65 kVp, 5–6 mAs. Cassette: 10 × 8 inches.

7️⃣ Humerus (Shaft)

  • AP erect: CP midway between elbow and shoulder joints. Lateral erect: second basic view; AP supine and lateral supine are listed as alternatives.
  • Position the arm as tolerated and include the entire humerus plus both shoulder and elbow joints.
  • In trauma, adapt the projection rather than forcing arm rotation; use multiple overlapping images if required by protocol to cover the whole bone.
  • Collimate to the full humerus and adjacent joints; check anatomy coverage, marker, motion and exposure.

Course factors: 65–70 kVp, 5.5–6 mAs. Cassette: 17 × 14 inches listed.

8️⃣ Shoulder

Course Views and Centring

  • AP erect (15° as written): CP at palpable coracoid process.
  • Supero-inferior axial: CP at mid-glenohumeral joint.
  • Supplementary views named: infero-superior/reverse axial, apical oblique (Garth), anterior oblique (Y), outlet projections for calcified tendons, AP and lateral outlet.

Positioning & Image Evaluation (Supplemental)

  • Position erect or supine as appropriate; centre the shoulder joint and follow the requested arm rotation and department protocol.
  • For suspected dislocation/trauma, use a safe trauma series. Do not move the arm into a painful rotation; a scapular Y or modified axial view may be selected by protocol.
  • Collimate to the proximal humerus and glenohumeral region.

Course factors: 60–75 kVp, 6–7 mAs. Exact cassette-size pairing unclear — verify original.

9️⃣ Clavicle and Scapula

Clavicle

  • Course CP: middle of clavicle. Source lists ‘PS’ and supplementary AP supine, infero-superior erect and infero-superior supine views.
  • Position erect or supine as tolerated; include the whole clavicle from sternoclavicular to acromioclavicular joints.
  • Some protocols use a cephalic CR angle to project the clavicle above ribs; angle and method vary — follow local protocol.

Course factors: 60–75 kVp, 8 mAs. Cassette: 12 × 10 inches.

Scapula

  • Course AP erect CP: 2.5 cm inferior to medial head of humerus (source wording). Lateral/anterior oblique CP: midpoint of medial border of scapula.
  • AP: abduct arm if safe to move scapula laterally. Lateral/oblique: rotate patient as tolerated to profile scapula.
  • Collimate to include the entire scapula, including acromion and inferior angle.

Course factors: 60–75 kVp, 7 mAs. Cassette: 12 × 10 inches.

General Image-Quality Checklist

  • Correct patient/side and projection
  • Required anatomy included
  • Correct marker
  • No significant motion
  • Appropriate exposure
  • Field appropriately collimated
  • Positioning matches the intended view

1️⃣1️⃣ Clinical Reasoning and Supplementary Views

  • Suspected fracture/dislocation: obtain requested orthogonal views; avoid unnecessary movement and adapt for trauma.
  • Suspected foreign body in hand: centre to the site and include it fully in appropriate projections.
  • Wrist pain / possible scaphoid injury: dedicated scaphoid views (ulnar deviation, oblique, Stecher, etc.) may be requested.
  • Elbow cannot extend: partial-flexion views may show distal humerus and proximal forearm without forcing extension.
  • Shoulder trauma: scapular Y, axial or modified axial projections may help assess alignment.
  • Clavicle/scapula injury: centre to the requested bone and include the whole structure; do not confuse these with a routine shoulder series.

For any supplementary view, explain: why it is requested, anatomy demonstrated, patient position, centring/CR, collimation, image criteria and safety considerations.

1️⃣3️⃣ Practice Questions and Final Checklist

  1. Describe a routine DP hand examination.
    Model: Verify request, identity and side; explain; position palm flat with fingers extended/separated; centre at head of 3rd metacarpal; collimate to hand; place marker; use approved factors; check coverage, rotation, motion and exposure.
  2. Describe AP forearm and centring.
    Model: Position AP as tolerated with shoulder, elbow and wrist aligned; include both elbow and wrist; centre midway between joints; collimate full forearm; assess rotation and coverage.
  3. Describe lateral elbow.
    Model: Flex to about 90° if tolerated, thumb up, align epicondyles for true lateral and centre to lateral epicondyle. Do not force flexion in trauma.
  4. State the course exposure factors.
    Model: Hand 50–55/2–3; wrist 50–55/2.5–4; forearm 60–65/5–6; elbow 60–65/5–6; humerus 65–70/5.5–6; shoulder 60–75/6–7; clavicle 60–75/8; scapula 60–75/7.
  5. How do you prevent repeats?
    Model: Check patient/side, positioning, centring, field, marker, exposure chart and motion instructions before exposing.
  6. What if the cassette-size entry is unclear?
    Model: Do not guess; verify the original course table or department protocol.

Final Checklist

  • Memorise the source centring points and exposure figures for all eight regions.
  • For every projection, describe indication, preparation, positioning, CP/CR, collimation, factors/SID if taught, marker and image criteria.
  • Remember to include both elbow and wrist joints for forearm and both shoulder and elbow joints for humerus.
  • Never force a painful or unstable limb into position.
  • If a source value is missing or unclear, say so and refer to the original table or approved local chart.

Projection and indication → Preparation and side → Positioning → Centring/CR → Collimation and SID if specified → Exposure chart → Image criteria → Safety.
