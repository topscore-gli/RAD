Topics

General Introduction & Filament Circuits

General Introduction to X-ray Equipment Engineering covers the fundamental components and principles involved in X-ray production. It explains the X-ray circuit, power supplies, SMPS, mains and filament circuits, transformers, rectifiers, control panel, X-ray tube, thermionic emission, and the roles of kVp, mA, and exposure time. The topic also introduces stationary and rotating anode tubes and explains how electrical energy is controlled and converted into X-rays.

Mains Circuit

The Mains Circuit is the part of an X-ray system responsible for supplying and controlling electrical power to create the potential difference between the cathode and anode. It covers mains supply voltages, different types of switches, circuit breakers, magnetic contactors, remote-controlled switches, and line voltage compensators used to maintain stable voltage for consistent X-ray production.

Mains Voltage Compensation

Mains Voltage Compensation is an important part of an X-ray machine circuit that helps maintain a stable voltage despite fluctuations in the electrical supply. This topic covers manual and automatic voltage compensation, as well as line-drop compensation during exposure. It explains how voltage changes and increased tube current can affect X-ray production, image quality, and equipment performance, and how compensating systems help maintain consistent and reliable X-ray output.

High Tension Cables, Plugs & Sockets

High-Tension Cables, Plugs and Sockets examines the construction, functions, and safety features of high-tension connections used in X-ray equipment. It covers the central conductors, insulation, earth cable braid, outer cable covering, high-tension plugs, sockets, and gland nuts, explaining how these components safely transmit high voltage between the generator and X-ray tube while minimizing voltage loss and preventing electrical leakage.

Fuses, Switches & Circuit Breakers

Fuses, Switches and Circuit Breakers explores the essential electrical protection and control devices used in X-ray equipment. It explains how fuses protect circuits from excessive current, the differences between slow-blow and fast-acting fuses, fuse components and ratings, and how thermal and electromagnetic circuit breakers operate. The topic also covers mains supply switches, knife switches, and magnetic relays used to control and isolate X-ray equipment safely.

High Tension Transformers

High-Tension Transformers are essential components of X-ray equipment that step up the relatively low mains voltage to the very high voltage required by the X-ray tube. This topic covers transformer construction, winding ratios, kVp and RMS voltage, core designs, transformer losses, cooling and insulation, voltage regulation, efficiency, and transformer ratings.

Interlocking Circuits

Interlocking Circuits are fail-safe mechanisms in X-ray equipment designed to prevent unsafe operation and protect the X-ray tube from damage. This topic covers tube stator interlocks, which ensure proper anode rotation before exposure, and overload interlocks, which prevent unsafe combinations of kV, mA, and exposure time. It also explains slug relay circuits, analog and mechanical interlocks, and the limitations of older protection systems.

Exposure Factor Selection

Exposure Factor Selection covers the selection and control of the main factors used in X-ray imaging: kilovoltage (kV), milliamperage (mA), and exposure time. It explains how kV affects X-ray photon energy and penetrating power, the operation of autotransformers and Variac transformers, and the causes of voltage loss during exposure.
